Understand the product · 1

1 · What Backup OS is

The problem

NIAT runs classes at 40+ partner universities. Sometimes an instructor is suddenly absent — they fall sick, or they get pulled onto other work. When that happens, a class must not be left without a teacher. Someone has to quickly arrange a backup instructor, decide whether they teach online or offline (on campus), make sure the session actually happens, and — if the backup travelled to a campus — settle their expenses within a day and get it approved.

Doing all of this over calls, chats and spreadsheets is slow and easy to lose track of. Nobody has one clear view of "which absences are open, who is covering them, and which payments are pending."

The solution

Backup OS is NIAT's internal web platform that runs this whole process in one place, with full accountability. It is part of NIAT's "OS" family of tools (alongside Campus OS and Teach OS).

  • A university reports an absence — it becomes a ticket.
  • The ticket automatically reaches the right people (Program Ops and the subject's Capability Manager).
  • A backup instructor is chosen and the mode (online/offline) is set.
  • The session is delivered.
  • If it was offline, the backup files a claim (invoice) which is approved by Ops and then the HOD.
  • Every step is recorded, and the right people are notified by in-app alert, email, and Microsoft Teams.
In one line Backup OS turns "an instructor is absent" into a tracked, accountable flow that ends in "the class was covered and everyone was paid and approved."

How access is decided

Access = role × scope. Scope is how wide you can see: Global (everything), University (one campus), or Capability (one subject vertical).

Auto sign-in by email If your email is already listed on a directory (as staff, CM, or a backup in the pool), you get the right role automatically the first time you sign in with Google. No waiting for a manual grant.

4 · The big picture — a ticket's life

Every absence follows the same path. Here it is, start to finish:

1Raised
2CM Review
3Mode set
4Backup Assigned
5Confirmed & Dispatched
6Session Done
7Invoice (offline)
8Ops Approved
9HOD Approved
10Closed

In plain words:

  1. Raised — a university staff member (or Ops) reports the absence. It instantly reaches Program Ops and the subject's Capability Manager.
  2. CM review — the CM (or Ops) looks at who is free and picks a backup.
  3. Mode set — Program Ops decides online or offline (on campus).
  4. Backup assigned — the chosen backup is attached to the ticket and notified.
  5. Confirmed & dispatched — Ops confirms; the backup is officially on, and the person who raised it is told.
  6. Session done — the class is delivered. If it was online, the ticket can now close.
  7. Invoice (offline only) — within 24 hours the backup files travel/stay/other expenses with proof.
  8. Ops approved — Program Ops checks and approves the claim.
  9. HOD approved — the HOD gives the final sign-off.
  10. Closed — settled and done. (A ticket can also be Cancelled if the backup is no longer needed.)
Two endings Online sessions finish quickly: Session Done → Closed. Offline sessions have the extra money trail: Invoice → Ops → HOD → Closed.

Every single step is written to the history log (who did it and when), so nothing is ever lost or unclear.

Routing map — who acts and who is told

At each stage, one role acts and the system automatically notifies the next people who need to know (in-app + email + Teams). This is the heart of "nothing falls through the cracks."

7 · Raising an absence ticket

Who does this: University Staff (for their campus) or Program Ops. Instructors cannot raise tickets. Most tickets actually arrive automatically from Zoho (see Section 21) — but you can also raise one by hand.

Steps

  1. Go to Operations → Tickets and click Raise ticket (top-right).
  2. Pick the University and the Subject.
  3. Choose the absent instructor, the reason (sick, assigned other work, etc.), and the dates/time of the absence.
  4. Optionally add notes and a suggested mode.
  5. Click Raise. The ticket is created and instantly routed to Ops and the subject's Capability Manager(s).
Example

Crescent University's staff member reports that Sanjay will be absent for Operating Systems on 18 Aug. They pick the university, the subject, the instructor, reason "Assigned other work," and the date, then click Raise. Ticket BIT-0017 is created and the Operating Systems Capability Manager is notified within seconds.

New or unknown subject? If the subject isn't in the list, choose "Other (not listed)". The ticket is still created and Program Ops is alerted to add the subject and its Capability Manager. See Section 17.
Do the work · 8

8 · Assigning a backup & picking the mode

Who does this: the Capability Manager and/or Program Ops. Open the ticket from the Tickets list to see its Next action panel on the right.

Steps

  1. Open a Raised ticket. The left side shows all the details; the right side is where you act.
  2. Under Backup instructor (from pool), pick a candidate. The system suggests good matches first and warns if someone is already busy or has a date clash. If the person isn't in the pool yet, use + Add to pool or type a name.
  3. Under Delivery mode, Program Ops chooses Online or Offline (on campus).
  4. Click Assign backup. The backup is attached and notified; the ticket moves to Backup Assigned.
The details you're working from The left panel shows the university, subject, capability, Capability Manager, the absent instructor, dates, requested mode, and who raised it. This is everything you need to make a good choice.
If the subject has no Capability Manager A special panel appears asking the admin to set up the capability + manager first. Once that's saved, the normal assign box appears. (More in Section 17.)
Do the work · 9

9 · Confirm & dispatch

Who does this: Program Ops. After a backup is assigned, Ops gives the final go-ahead.

  1. Open the Backup Assigned ticket.
  2. Review the choice, then click Confirm & dispatch.
  3. The ticket becomes Confirmed. The backup is officially on, and the person who raised the ticket is automatically notified.
Why a separate confirm step? It's the moment of commitment. Assigning is a proposal; confirming is "yes, this is happening" — and that's when the raiser and backup get the green light.

After the class is taught, the ticket moves to Session Done. For online sessions, that's essentially the end — it can be closed. For offline sessions, the money trail begins (next section).

10 · The session & the invoice (offline)

Who does this: the backup instructor. This only applies to offline (on-campus) sessions, where the backup had real expenses.

The 24-hour rule

The claim window opens at 4 PM (IST) on the last day of the absence and stays open for 24 hours. The backup must file the claim inside that window.

Filing the claim

  1. Open the ticket (or go to My Assignments) and find the Invoice & approvals panel.
  2. Enter the Session end date and a short description.
  3. Fill the claim breakdown: Travel, Accommodation, and Other. The total adds up live.
  4. Paste the NxtClaim link (required).
  5. Upload the charge slips — up to 5 images/PDFs as proof.
  6. Submit. The ticket moves to Invoice Pending and Program Ops is notified to review.
Reminders & red flags While the window is open, Backup OS pings the backup on Teams (repeating every few hours) to "upload your invoice." If the 24 hours pass with no claim, the ticket gets a red flag and the upload is locked.
Missed the window? (late upload) After the deadline the backup can't upload. The panel shows a note to contact Admin/CM. An admin or HOD can press Approve late upload, which re-opens the window and notifies the backup so they can still file.
Example

Sparsh Sharma covered an offline Frontend session at Chaitanya University for absent instructor Attar Asif (ticket BIT-0031). After the class, Sparsh opens the ticket, enters travel ₹700 + accommodation ₹0 + other ₹300 = ₹1,000, pastes the NxtClaim link, uploads the bus ticket photo, and submits — all within 24 hours. The ticket becomes Invoice Pending.

11 · Approvals — Ops then HOD

Offline claims are approved by two people, in order. This separation means no one can approve their own money.

Step 1 — Program Ops approves

  1. Ops opens the Invoice Pending ticket (or the Invoices page).
  2. They check the amount, the breakdown, the NxtClaim link and the charge slips.
  3. They click Approve → the ticket becomes Ops Approved and the HOD is notified. (Or Return it for a fix.)

Step 2 — HOD gives final sign-off

  1. The claim lands in the HOD Approvals queue (Operations menu).
  2. The HOD verifies the same details.
  3. They click Approve → the ticket becomes HOD Approved, then Closed. (Or Return.)
Done Once the HOD approves and the ticket closes, the absence is fully handled and settled. It now shows in Analytics and the history log.

17 · Capability Managers & new subjects

This one page manages two linked things: the subject verticals (capabilities) and the managers who own them. When a ticket lands for a subject, everyone listed here for that subject's vertical is notified (Teams @mention + email + in-app).

  • The top Subject verticals panel lets you create, rename, or retire a vertical.
  • The table below lists the Capability Managers, each with their email (which is also their login) and the vertical they own. A vertical can have several managers.

When a brand-new subject appears

Sometimes a university reports an absence for a subject that isn't set up yet. Backup OS handles this safely:

  1. The ticket still gets created and is marked ⚠ New subject — needs admin. Program Ops is alerted on Teams and by email.
  2. The admin comes to this page and — in this order — first adds the new subject vertical (top panel), then adds the Capability Manager(s) for it (the table below), choosing that new vertical.
  3. The order matters: the manager's form has a required "Capability" dropdown that only lists verticals that already exist — so the vertical must be created first.
  4. As soon as both exist, the managers are notified and the ticket flows normally.
It wires itself up The moment a manager is added, their login role, the "lead" shown on tickets/cards, and all notifications for that subject start working automatically — no extra setup.

21 · Where tickets come from (Zoho)

Universities already report things through Zoho forms. Backup OS is connected to Zoho so that when a university submits a "Backup Instructor Required" request, a ticket appears here automatically — no double entry.

  • The connection is read-only from Zoho into Backup OS: tickets flow in, we never write back into Zoho's records.
  • The university is taken from the submitter's staff profile; the subject and instructor come straight from the form.
  • If the subject is new, the Zoho form offers "Other (not listed)", which triggers the new-subject alert (Section 17).
  • Tickets that came from Zoho are marked as such in the history (e.g. "Raised via Zoho").
Why this matters Staff keep using the form they already know, and Ops still gets one clean, tracked queue inside Backup OS.

24 · Frequently asked questions

I signed in but I can't see anything. Why?

Your email isn't linked to a role yet. Ask Program Ops to add you on the Access page, or your CM to add you to the Backup Pool with your email, then use Re-check my access.

Can an instructor raise a ticket?

No — raising is done by University Staff or Program Ops. Instructors see only their own assignments.

What if the class was online?

No invoice is needed. The ticket goes straight from Session Done to Closed.

I'm a backup and I missed the 24-hour window.

Contact Admin or your CM. An admin/HOD can approve a late upload, which re-opens the window for you.

A subject isn't in the list.

Choose "Other (not listed)" when raising. Program Ops will add the subject and its Capability Manager, then the ticket continues.

Who approves the money?

Two people, in order: Program Ops first, then the HOD. The same person can never do both.

Where can I see everything that happened to a ticket?

Open the ticket (its timeline), or use the Logs page for the full history.

Do I have to check the app all day?

No. Backup OS notifies the right person by in-app bell, email, and Teams @mention whenever it's their turn.

How a ticket "routes" through the app

Putting the two ideas together — the stages and the pages:

  1. A ticket is born on /dashboard/tickets (by hand via Raise ticket, or automatically from Zoho).
  2. It is worked on its own page /dashboard/tickets/<id> — assign, set mode, confirm — with each action notifying the next role.
  3. Offline claims then appear on /dashboard/invoices for Ops, and on /dashboard/hod-approvals for the HOD.
  4. Every action along the way is recorded on /dashboard/logs, and the numbers roll up into /dashboard/analytics.
The whole product in one sentence Absence → ticket on the Tickets page → routed to Ops + the subject's Capability Managers → backup & mode set → confirmed → delivered → (offline) claim on Invoices → Ops → HOD Approvals → closed, with notifications and a full log at every step.
